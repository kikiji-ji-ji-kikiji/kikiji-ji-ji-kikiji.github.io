This dissertation examines how the rollout of South Korea's high-speed rail network has reshaped regional development outcomes in small and medium-sized cities. Using the Callaway-Sant'Anna staggered Difference-in-Differences framework, the analysis addresses the heterogeneous timing of HSR station openings across cities between 2008 and 2020, avoiding the bias that standard two-way fixed effects models introduce under staggered treatment adoption.

The research combines panel economic indicators with spatial and transport network data to estimate treatment effect heterogeneity across city size, geographic location, and time since treatment, contributing to the broader literature on transport infrastructure and regional economic geography.
