Fear of Living an Average Life

Every morning, I wake up to the same routine: the alarm blares, I grab an Americano from the café, and sit in front of a screen. The world inside my phone buzzes with life story after story. As I scroll, a gnawing fear grips my heart: the fear of living an average life.

I watched people chasing their dreams while feeling tethered to mediocrity. Every day became a constant reminder of unfulfilled potential. Eventually, it led to mental exhaustion and a body that felt perpetually tired. This daily battle within myself finally forced me to pause my mundane routine. In that pause came an epiphany, one that made me reevaluate the direction of my life. A journey of self-discovery began, with a vow to transform my once-average life into something I would truly cherish. The adventure had begun.

When I look back, I see someone who was possibly living one of the best lives anyone could imagine and yet, here I am, someone who actually lived it. I grew up with perfect mental peace, full of confidence, flying without wings, unafraid of failure, with the freedom to do whatever I wanted, even in a South Asian household. I was surrounded by books of every kind, which later became the foundation of my deepest understanding of human behavior.

And yet, at the age of twenty-five, I found myself trapped in mental torment. I was calculating every moment, every feeling, every circumstance, all of which pushed me toward a terrible fear, a phobia of mundaneness. After enough reflection, the realization struck me: it was all coming from comparison.

I was comparing my worth and competence with everyone around me. I was only taking my first steps while everyone else seemed to be nearing the finish line. It was an unfair comparison, one that was deeply unkind to my soul.

How did I even learn to compare myself with others? Nobody taught me this at home, nor did I learn it in school. I learned it from a world of competition, where everyone was running toward… what? Maybe stability? Recognition?

And I guess I cannot calm my brain until I fully understand what and why’s. So I guess I will explore until then.

And yes! the journey begins.

A journey of self-reclamation. A journey to rediscover and hold on to the inner peace I always had. A journey toward struggles that I will claim as entirely my own.

This autumn brought me back to myself. ❤
